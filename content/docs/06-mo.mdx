The Marine Operations Manager view exists to protect the coral reef, Red Sea Global's most valuable natural asset. Reef damage tends to happen quietly, and by the time it is visible it is usually permanent, so the only effective protection is early warning. This view provides that warning in three ways. It gives live sight of reef turbidity and temperature. It shows how many boats and divers are near the reef at any moment, so the site never becomes overcrowded. And when a threshold is crossed, it raises the alarm and assigns the response automatically, rather than leaving it to manual spotting and coordination.

![The wide view of the destination waters. The bottom strip is the marine read: reef conditions, mooring, dive operation, and wind.](06-mo-lod1-1.jpg)

## What you see

At the wide view, the bottom strip is the marine read for the destination waters, grouped into reef conditions, capacity management, and active response. A map legend in the corner counts the open items by severity and confirms the environmental sensor feed is live.

| Group | Field | Reads | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reef conditions | Water turbidity | 2.1 NTU, Stable | Reef water cloudiness, trended against threshold |
| Reef conditions | Reef temperature | 22 C, Stable | Sea temperature, trended against bleach-risk threshold |
| Capacity management | Boats near reef | 9 of 12 | Boats at the reef against capacity |
| Capacity management | Divers near reef | 26 of 40, across 5 sites | Divers down against capacity, spread over the open sites |
| Capacity management | Capacity-aware mooring allocation | bar | Moorings occupied against those available |
| Active response | Threshold alarms | All clear | Whether turbidity, temperature, and capacity sit within thresholds |
| Active response | Dynamic dive-site switching | Recommended action | A suggested reroute when a cluster is better served elsewhere |
| Active response | Task triggered | Dispatch patrol boat, notify dive operator | The security and operations tasks a recommendation raises |

The map legend reads the open items by severity: 0 critical, 1 high, 2 medium, and 8 low or info, fed by the environmental sensor feed running live.


## Reading reef conditions across the waters

The read works at two levels. At the wide view the strip covers the destination waters. Drop into a marine zone, here the Shura Marina dive and mooring water, and the read rescopes to that water, where boat and diver counts against capacity become legible.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Problem | Reef damage tends to happen quietly, and by the time it is visible it is usually permanent. |
| Goal | Hold live sight of reef turbidity and temperature, and of the boats and divers near the reef, so crowding or a condition shift can be caught before it does physical harm. |
| Precondition | The marine feed is live: reef conditions, mooring, dive operation, and wind are reporting. |
| Steps | 1. Open the Marine Operations Manager from the persona bar.<br/>2. Read the marine strip: reef conditions, mooring, dive operation, and wind.<br/>3. Select a marine zone to inspect dive and mooring capacity.<br/>4. Watch turbidity and temperature against their thresholds, and counts against capacity. |
| Postcondition | Crowding or a reef-condition shift is visible in real time, before it does any physical harm. |

## The close-up: active response

At the closest level the view drills into a reef site. Conditions can read all clear, turbidity, temperature, and capacity all within thresholds, and the view still surfaces a recommendation when a cluster would be better served at another site. The recommendation, once applied, raises the security and operations tasks behind it rather than leaving them to manual coordination.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Problem | Spreading boats and divers well across the reef meant watching capacity by hand and coordinating any reroute manually, even when nothing had breached a threshold. |
| Goal | Surface a dive-site switch while conditions are still all clear, and on apply, raise the patrol and dive-operator tasks automatically so the reroute is already moving. |
| Precondition | Reef conditions, capacity, and the dive-site network are reporting, with thresholds reading all clear. |
| Steps | 1. The threshold alarms read all clear: turbidity, temperature, and capacity within thresholds.<br/>2. The recommended action surfaces: divert the Shebara Cluster to Shebara Wall.<br/>3. Apply the action, or defer with Remind me later.<br/>4. On apply, the task triggers: dispatch a patrol boat and notify the dive operator, tagged to security and operations. |
| Postcondition | The cluster is rerouted to Shebara Wall, with the patrol and dive-operator tasks already assigned, before any threshold is approached. |

![Close-up of Shura Marina. With threshold alarms reading all clear, the recommended action diverts the Shebara Cluster to Shebara Wall, and on apply triggers a patrol boat dispatch and a dive-operator notification, tagged to security and operations.](06-mo-lod2-3.jpg)


## Working in this view

1. Open the Marine Operations Manager from the persona bar.
2. Read reef conditions, mooring, and dive operation across the waters at the wide view.
3. Select a marine zone to inspect dive and mooring capacity.
4. Open a reef site to see the threshold breach and the automatic response.
