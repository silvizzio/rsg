The Marine Operations Manager view helps protect the coral reef by monitoring turbidity, temperature, and the number of boats and divers at each site. Early warning matters because reef damage may be difficult to see as it develops and can be lasting. When a threshold is crossed, the system raises an alarm and automatically assigns the response.

![The wide view of the destination waters. The bottom strip is the marine summary: reef conditions, mooring, dive operation, and wind.](06-mo-lod1-1.jpg)

## The close-up: three things to act on

At site level, a recommended-action card can flag a local issue even when the destination summary reads All clear. Each card describes the condition, includes a live feed for checking the site, and offers a primary action or Remind me later. The examples below cover three alert types.

### Resting a site before a temperature breach

One site can approach its bleaching-risk temperature threshold while readings elsewhere remain within limits. The card recommends resting the affected site and moving groups before that threshold is reached.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Temp rising (amber) |
| Why it matters | Groups may remain at a site while its temperature approaches the bleaching-risk threshold. |
| What to check | Rest the affected site and move groups while its temperature is still within the limit. |
| Before you start | Per-site reef temperature is reporting against a threshold, here 30.5 C at Site B. |
| What to do | 1. The card shows: water temp 29.8 C trending toward the 30.5 threshold at Site B.<br/>2. Confirm the Dive Site B feed, camera DB-02, inside the card.<br/>3. Rest Site B and shift afternoon groups to Site C, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Expected result | Site B is rested and afternoon groups are moved to Site C before the forecast threshold crossing. |

![Close-up of North Reef. With conditions reading all clear, the amber card rests Site B and shifts afternoon groups to Site C, because water temperature is 29.8 C trending toward the 30.5 threshold and resting the site ahead of breach protects coral recovery.](06-mo-lod3-reef-health-monitoring.jpg)

### Holding arrivals to keep a site within capacity

When a site is close to its boat or mooring limit, further arrivals could put it over capacity. Hold those arrivals and redirect them to another site.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Capacity watch (amber) |
| Why it matters | Further arrivals can push a busy site beyond its boat or mooring capacity. |
| What to check | Hold new arrivals and redirect them to keep the site within its limit. |
| Before you start | Per-site boat and mooring counts are reporting against capacity, here North Reef. |
| What to do | 1. The card shows: 11 of 12 boats and 17 of 18 moorings in use at North Reef.<br/>2. Confirm the North Reef feed, camera NR-02, inside the card.<br/>3. Hold new arrivals at North Reef and route them to Site B, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Expected result | New arrivals are redirected to Site B to keep North Reef within capacity. |

![Close-up of Shura Marina. The amber card holds new arrivals at North Reef and routes them to Site B, because 11 of 12 boats and 17 of 18 moorings are in use and holding arrivals keeps North Reef within capacity.](06-mo-lod3-capacity-management.jpg)

### Diverting on a threshold breach

When a threshold is breached, the card raises an alarm and automatically assigns a diversion task to marine security. Review the reading and live feed to confirm the situation.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Threshold breach (red) |
| Why it matters | Manual detection and coordination can delay the response to a threshold breach. |
| What to check | Raise the alarm and automatically assign the diversion task to marine security. |
| Before you start | Per-site turbidity is reporting against a threshold, here 5.0 NTU at North Reef, with marine security on call for auto-assignment. |

Divert and assign: the task to move dive groups from North Reef to Site C is auto-assigned to marine security, or defer with Remind me later. |

## Simulations this view runs

Marine Operations has six simulations. The platform groups Wave and Ocean current under Traffic; Flood and storm surge under Transport; and Wind, Water quality, and Coral reef under Environment. The simulation object is fixed to Vessels. Marine runs model environmental conditions and vessels, but do not model swimmers or divers as agents.

Use these simulations to assess whether marine activities can proceed and what restrictions or preparations may be needed.

| Category | Simulation | What it shows | Decisions it supports | Details |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traffic | Wave | Wave height against the small-craft limit, and exposed vessels along their routes | Plan departure holds and beach calls around wave conditions | [Wave](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#wave__marine-operations-manager) |
| Traffic | Ocean current | Drift at the channel and along the dive routes, and the vessels in the drift zone | Adjust dive routing and vessel calls before boats are committed | [Ocean current](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#ocean-current__marine-operations-manager) |
| Transport | Flood and storm surge | Surge height, speed, and arrival time at the berths | Plan when to secure or relocate craft and raise barriers | [Flood and storm surge](/docs/10-simulation-transport#flood-and-storm-surge__marine-operations-manager) |
| Environment | Wind | The marine window, the small-craft limit, and mooring exposure | Set departure windows and secure moorings before conditions tighten | [Wind](/docs/11-simulation-environment#wind__marine-operations-manager) |
| Environment | Water quality | Dive clarity, salinity, temperature, and dissolved oxygen at the dive sites | Review diving and swimming plans before guests enter the water | [Water quality](/docs/11-simulation-environment#water-quality__marine-operations-manager) |
| Environment | Coral reef | Bleaching risk against the closure trigger, with anchor and boat pressure | Plan site rest or closure before the trigger is reached | [Coral reef](/docs/11-simulation-environment#coral-reef__marine-operations-manager) |

### Traffic

**Wave.** Use the wave simulation to assess when conditions on vessel routes and at the marina approach the small-craft limit. It helps plan departures and beach calls within the available operating window. The forecast follows offshore swell towards the marina and along the routes, showing wave height and where waves break. Review exposed sections before deciding whether to hold a departure or change a beach call. [Read in full](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#wave__marine-operations-manager)

![Wave, Marine Operations view. Wave height 1.6m against the small-craft limit, 6 vessels exposed, marina surge watch.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-wave.jpg)

**Ocean current.** Use the current simulation to assess drift risk at the channel and along dive routes before boats depart. Conditions at a particular site can differ from the average across the destination. The forecast models the selected tide and wind conditions and shows where currents strengthen. Use those local readings to review dive routing and vessel movements before committing boats to a route. [Read in full](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#ocean-current__marine-operations-manager)

![Ocean current, Marine Operations view. Drift risk at the channel, current 0.6 m/s, 5 vessels in the drift zone, handling harder.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-ocean-current.jpg)

### Transport

**Flood and storm surge.** Use this simulation to assess the effect of a storm, tide, and rainfall scenario on vessels and the marina. The aim is to plan when craft should be secured or relocated and when barriers should be raised. The forecast shows surge height at the berths, water speed, and arrival time. Compare those conditions with the time needed to prepare the marina and move vessels before the peak. [Read in full](/docs/10-simulation-transport#flood-and-storm-surge__marine-operations-manager)

![Flood and storm surge, Marine Operations view. Surge at the marina 1.8m, 210 guests and 24 vehicles in the depth zone, surge arrival 18 min.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-flood-and-storm-surge.jpg)

### Environment

**Wind.** Use the wind simulation to assess the operating window for diving and vessel departures, along with exposure at moorings. It helps the team plan the day around the small-craft limit and other operating limits. The forecast models wind speed and direction over the resort and water, including gusts and mooring exposure. Review it when setting departure windows and deciding when to secure moorings. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#wind__marine-operations-manager)

![Wind, Marine Operations view. Marine wind window 14 kn, small-craft limit clear, mooring exposure low.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-wind.jpg)

**Water quality.** Marine Operations uses this simulation to assess water conditions for planned diving and swimming activities. It helps the team review the schedule before guests enter the water. The forecast models discharge and flushing at dive sites and shows clarity, salinity, temperature, and dissolved oxygen. If visibility is expected to fall below the activity threshold, review the timing or choice of site. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#water-quality__marine-operations-manager)

![Water quality, Marine Operations view. Dive clarity 7.0m visibility below the activity threshold, DO low, activity limited.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-water-quality.jpg)

**Coral reef.** Marine Operations uses this simulation to plan when a dive site should be rested or closed as heat stress increases. The forecast compares bleaching risk with the closure trigger and includes the additional pressure from anchors and boats. Use it to plan site rotation and protection while risk is still building. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#coral-reef__marine-operations-manager)

![Coral reef, Marine Operations view. Bleaching risk 5.2 DHW approaching the closure trigger, reef health good, anchor risk low.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-coral.jpg)

## Working in this view

1. Sign in to the Marine Operations Manager view using your assigned account.
2. Read reef conditions, mooring, and dive operation across the waters at the wide view.
3. Select a marine zone to inspect dive and mooring capacity.
4. Open a reef site to read its recommended action.
5. Move through the virtual tour to inspect the reef and open a buoy status card.

<Callout type="note">
Air and weather readings from the Environment Manager view are read-only in this view.
</Callout>
