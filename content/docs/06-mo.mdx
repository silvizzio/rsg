The Marine Operations Manager view exists to protect the coral reef, Red Sea Global's most valuable natural asset. Reef damage tends to happen quietly, and by the time it is visible it is usually permanent, so the only effective protection is early warning. This view provides that warning in three ways. It gives live sight of reef turbidity and temperature. It shows how many boats and divers are near the reef at any moment, so the site never becomes overcrowded. And when a threshold is crossed, it raises the alarm and assigns the response automatically, rather than leaving it to manual spotting and coordination.

![The wide view of the destination waters. The bottom strip is the marine read: reef conditions, mooring, dive operation, and wind.](06-mo-lod1-1.jpg)

## The close-up: three things to act on

At the closest level the view drills into a reef site. The destination strip can read all clear in aggregate while a single site surfaces a condition worth acting on, so the close-up is where those site-level cards appear. Each card states the situation, carries the live feed for the site so it can be confirmed without leaving the card, and offers a primary action plus Remind me later. Three recur, across three alert tiers.

### Resting a site before a temperature breach

Reef temperature can climb toward its bleach-risk threshold at one site while the rest of the water stays comfortable. Resting the site before the breach, rather than after, is what protects coral recovery.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Temp rising (amber) |
| Problem | A site could cross its temperature threshold before anyone moved groups off it, by which point the heat stress on the coral had already begun. |
| Goal | Catch a site trending toward its temperature threshold and rest it ahead of the breach, shifting groups elsewhere while conditions are still within limits. |
| Precondition | Per-site reef temperature is reporting against a threshold, here 30.5 C at Site B. |
| Steps | 1. The card surfaces: water temp 29.8 C trending toward the 30.5 threshold at Site B.<br/>2. Confirm the Dive Site B feed, camera DB-02, inside the card.<br/>3. Rest Site B and shift afternoon groups to Site C, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | Site B is rested ahead of the breach and afternoon groups move to Site C, protecting coral recovery before any heat stress sets in. |

![Close-up of North Reef. With conditions reading all clear, the amber card rests Site B and shifts afternoon groups to Site C, because water temperature is 29.8 C trending toward the 30.5 threshold and resting the site ahead of breach protects coral recovery.](06-mo-lod3-reef-health-monitoring.jpg)

### Holding arrivals to keep a site within capacity

A reef site fills before it is unsafe, and the risk is that one more arrival tips it over capacity. Holding new arrivals and routing them elsewhere keeps the site within its limit without turning anyone away from the water.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Capacity watch (amber) |
| Problem | A site could be pushed past its boat and mooring capacity by arrivals that no one was holding back, crowding the reef. |
| Goal | Catch a site approaching capacity and hold new arrivals, routing them to another site so the reef stays within its limit. |
| Precondition | Per-site boat and mooring counts are reporting against capacity, here North Reef. |
| Steps | 1. The card surfaces: 11 of 12 boats and 17 of 18 moorings in use at North Reef.<br/>2. Confirm the North Reef feed, camera NR-02, inside the card.<br/>3. Hold new arrivals at North Reef and route them to Site B, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | New arrivals are held and routed to Site B, keeping North Reef within capacity rather than tipping it over. |

![Close-up of Shura Marina. The amber card holds new arrivals at North Reef and routes them to Site B, because 11 of 12 boats and 17 of 18 moorings are in use and holding arrivals keeps North Reef within capacity.](06-mo-lod3-capacity-management.jpg)

### Diverting on a threshold breach

When a site does cross a threshold, the response should already be moving by the time an operator looks. The breach card raises the alarm and auto-assigns the task to marine security rather than waiting for a manual dispatch.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Threshold breach (red) |
| Problem | Dealing with a breach meant spotting it and then coordinating a response by hand, losing time while the reef sat over threshold. |
| Goal | On a threshold breach, raise the alarm and auto-assign the divert to marine security, so the response is already moving when an operator looks. |
| Precondition | Per-site turbidity is reporting against a threshold, here 5.0 NTU at North Reef, with marine security on call for auto-assignment. |
| Steps | 1. The card fires: turbidity 5.2 NTU over the 5.0 threshold with 11 boats at the reef.<br/>2. Confirm the North Reef feed, camera NR-02, inside the card.<br/>3. Divert and assign: the task to move dive groups from North Reef to Site C is auto-assigned to marine security, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | Dive groups are diverted from North Reef to Site C with the task already assigned to marine security, without manual spotting or coordination. |

![Close-up of North Reef. The red card diverts dive groups from North Reef to Site C, because turbidity has reached 5.2 NTU over the 5.0 threshold with 11 boats at the reef, and the task is auto-assigned to marine security.](06-mo-lod3-active-response.jpg)

## Simulations this view runs

Marine Operations runs six simulations, grouped in the rail the way the platform presents them: Wave and Ocean current under Traffic, Flood and storm surge under Transport, and Wind, Water quality, and Coral reef under Environment. Across all six the simulation object is locked to Vessels. In-water people modelling is not in scope, so a marine run couples an environmental field to vessels on the water, never to swimmers or divers as agents.

Read as a set, each run turns an environmental field into a single operability call.

| Category | Simulation | What it reads | What it drives | Read in full |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traffic | Wave | Wave height against the small-craft limit, and the vessels exposed on the lines boats run | Hold departures and beach calls before a boat is caught exposed | [Wave](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#wave__marine-operations-manager) |
| Traffic | Ocean current | Drift at the channel and along the dive routes, and the vessels in the drift zone | Adjust dive routing and vessel calls before boats are committed | [Ocean current](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#ocean-current__marine-operations-manager) |
| Transport | Flood and storm surge | Surge height, speed, and arrival time at the berths | Secure or relocate craft and raise barriers while it still helps | [Flood and storm surge](/docs/10-simulation-transport#flood-and-storm-surge__marine-operations-manager) |
| Environment | Wind | The marine window, the small-craft limit, and mooring exposure | Set departure windows and secure moorings before conditions tighten | [Wind](/docs/11-simulation-environment#wind__marine-operations-manager) |
| Environment | Water quality | Dive clarity, salinity, temperature, and dissolved oxygen at the dive sites | Call the day's diving and swimming before guests are in the water | [Water quality](/docs/11-simulation-environment#water-quality__marine-operations-manager) |
| Environment | Coral reef | Bleaching risk against the closure trigger, with anchor and boat pressure | Rest or close a dive site deliberately, ahead of the trigger | [Coral reef](/docs/11-simulation-environment#coral-reef__marine-operations-manager) |

### Traffic

**Wave.** The goal is to keep small craft running safely while the window allows, and to call the hold early enough that no boat is caught exposed. The twin plays an offshore swell scenario forward to the marina and the routes, showing where wave height climbs toward the small-craft limit and where waves break on the lines boats actually run, so departures and beach calls are deliberate rather than last-minute. [Read in full](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#wave__marine-operations-manager)

![Wave, Marine Operations read. Wave height 1.6m against the small-craft limit, 6 vessels exposed, marina surge watch.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-wave.jpg)

**Ocean current.** The goal is a safe dive day: knowing when flow at the channel strengthens enough to make drift a real hazard, before boats are out. The twin plays a tidal and wind scenario forward to show how current builds at the channel and along the dive routes, separating the site reading from the broad average that hides it, so routing and vessel calls can be adjusted before boats are committed. [Read in full](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#ocean-current__marine-operations-manager)

![Ocean current, Marine Operations read. Drift risk at the channel, current 0.6 m/s, 5 vessels in the drift zone, handling harder.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-ocean-current.jpg)

### Transport

**Flood and storm surge.** The goal is to protect vessels and the marina, getting craft secured or relocated and barriers raised before the peak puts moored and small boats at risk. The twin plays a storm, tide, and rainfall scenario forward to the marina, reading how high the surge climbs at the berths, how fast water moves through them, and how soon it arrives, so the marine response is made with time in hand. [Read in full](/docs/10-simulation-transport#flood-and-storm-surge__marine-operations-manager)

![Flood and storm surge, Marine Operations read. Surge at the marina 1.8m, 210 guests and 24 vehicles in the depth zone, surge arrival 18 min.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-flood-and-storm-surge.jpg)

### Environment

**Wind.** The goal is to set the working day with confidence, knowing the marine window, the small-craft limit, and the load on moorings before conditions tighten. The twin plays a wind speed and direction scenario forward over the resort and the water, reading the window, the gusts, and the mooring exposure against their limits, so departure windows and moorings are set while conditions still allow. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#wind__marine-operations-manager)

![Wind, Marine Operations read. Marine wind window 14 kn, small-craft limit clear, mooring exposure low.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-wind.jpg)

**Water quality.** The goal is a clear call on whether the day's diving and swimming can go ahead, made before guests are in the water. The twin plays a discharge and flushing scenario forward at the dive sites, reading clarity, salinity, temperature, and dissolved oxygen together, so a clarity drop surfaces ahead of time and site choice can adapt before the activity call is forced. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#water-quality__marine-operations-manager)

![Water quality, Marine Operations read. Dive clarity 7.0m visibility below the activity threshold, DO low, activity limited.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-water-quality.jpg)

**Coral reef.** The goal is dive-site management: getting ahead of the heat stress that pushes a site toward the point where it should be rested or closed. The twin plays a heat-stress scenario forward and reads bleaching risk at the dive sites against the closure trigger, with the anchor and boat pressure that compounds it, so rotation and protection are planned while the risk is still building. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#coral-reef__marine-operations-manager)

![Coral reef, Marine Operations read. Bleaching risk 5.2 DHW approaching the closure trigger, reef health good, anchor risk low.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-coral.jpg)

## Working in this view

1. Open the Marine Operations Manager from the persona bar.
2. Read reef conditions, mooring, and dive operation across the waters at the wide view.
3. Select a marine zone to inspect dive and mooring capacity.
4. Open a reef site to read its recommended action.
5. Move through the virtual tour to inspect the reef and open a buoy status card.
