The Marine Operations Manager view exists to protect the coral reef, Red Sea Global's most valuable natural asset. Reef damage tends to happen quietly, and by the time it is visible it is usually permanent, so the only effective protection is early warning. This view provides that warning in three ways. It gives live sight of reef turbidity and temperature. It shows how many boats and divers are near the reef at any moment, so the site never becomes overcrowded. And when a threshold is crossed, it raises the alarm and assigns the response automatically, rather than leaving it to manual spotting and coordination.

## The close-up: three things to act on

At the closest level the view drills into a reef site. The destination strip can read all clear in aggregate while a single site surfaces a condition worth acting on, so the close-up is where those site-level cards appear. Each card states the situation, carries the live feed for the site so it can be confirmed without leaving the card, and offers a primary action plus Remind me later. Three recur, across three alert tiers.

### Resting a site before a temperature breach

Reef temperature can climb toward its bleach-risk threshold at one site while the rest of the water stays comfortable. Resting the site before the breach, rather than after, is what protects coral recovery.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Temp rising (amber) |
| Problem | A site could cross its temperature threshold before anyone moved groups off it, by which point the heat stress on the coral had already begun. |
| Goal | Catch a site trending toward its temperature threshold and rest it ahead of the breach, shifting groups elsewhere while conditions are still within limits. |
| Precondition | Per-site reef temperature is reporting against a threshold, here 30.5 C at Site B. |
| Steps | 1. The card surfaces: water temp 29.8 C trending toward the 30.5 threshold at Site B.<br/>2. Confirm the Dive Site B feed, camera DB-02, inside the card.<br/>3. Rest Site B and shift afternoon groups to Site C, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | Site B is rested ahead of the breach and afternoon groups move to Site C, protecting coral recovery before any heat stress sets in. |

![Close-up of North Reef. ### Holding arrivals to keep a site within capacity

A reef site fills before it is unsafe, and the risk is that one more arrival tips it over capacity. Holding new arrivals and routing them elsewhere keeps the site within its limit without turning anyone away from the water.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Capacity watch (amber) |
| Problem | A site could be pushed past its boat and mooring capacity by arrivals that no one was holding back, crowding the reef. |
| Goal | Catch a site approaching capacity and hold new arrivals, routing them to another site so the reef stays within its limit. |
| Precondition | Per-site boat and mooring counts are reporting against capacity, here North Reef. |
| Steps | 1. The card surfaces: 11 of 12 boats and 17 of 18 moorings in use at North Reef.<br/>2. Confirm the North Reef feed, camera NR-02, inside the card.<br/>3. Hold new arrivals at North Reef and route them to Site B, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | New arrivals are held and routed to Site B, keeping North Reef within capacity rather than tipping it over. |

![Close-up of Shura Marina. The amber card holds new arrivals at North Reef and routes them to Site B, because 11 of 12 boats and 17 of 18 moorings are in use and holding arrivals keeps North Reef within capacity.](06-mo-lod3-capacity-management.jpg)

### Diverting on a threshold breach

When a site does cross a threshold, the response should already be moving by the time an operator looks. The breach card raises the alarm and auto-assigns the task to marine security rather than waiting for a manual dispatch.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Tier | Threshold breach (red) |
| Problem | Dealing with a breach meant spotting it and then coordinating a response by hand, losing time while the reef sat over threshold. |
| Goal | On a threshold breach, raise the alarm and auto-assign the divert to marine security, so the response is already moving when an operator looks. |
| Precondition | Per-site turbidity is reporting against a threshold, here 5.0 NTU at North Reef, with marine security on call for auto-assignment. |
| Steps | 1. The card fires: turbidity 5.2 NTU over the 5.0 threshold with 11 boats at the reef.<br/>2. Confirm the North Reef feed, camera NR-02, inside the card.<br/>3. Divert and assign: the task to move dive groups from North Reef to Site C is auto-assigned to marine security, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | Dive groups are diverted from North Reef to Site C with the task already assigned to marine security, without manual spotting or coordination. |

![Close-up of North Reef. The red card diverts dive groups from North Reef to Site C, because turbidity has reached 5.2 NTU over the 5.0 threshold with 11 boats at the reef, and the task is auto-assigned to marine security.](06-mo-lod3-active-response.jpg)
