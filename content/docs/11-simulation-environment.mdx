The Environment domain covers the simulations that read the natural systems around the destination: the clarity and condition of the water, how it flushes and flows, the energy the sea carries to the shore, the heat stress sitting on the reef, and, for Marine Operations, the wind over the water. Each persona below runs the sims relevant to its remit, read against its own goal.

The scope label in the tool reads Shura Island as a placeholder and is not load-bearing in these descriptions.

## Wind

How wind moves across the resort and the water, read by each persona that runs it.

### Operations Manager

The operations manager's goal with wind is to call the safety thresholds deliberately, holding or closing exposed activities and operations before the wind crosses the line rather than after. Success is a closure sequenced ahead of the hazard, not a scramble once it has already arrived.

The twin gives that lead time as a hazard map. It plays a wind scenario forward, reading speed, gusts, and turbulence against the thresholds that trigger a restriction. The value is the chance to sequence holds and closures with margin, turning a weather call from a reactive judgement into a planned, defensible decision.

![Wind, Operations read.](placeholder-ops-wind.jpg)

### Marine Operations Manager

The marine operations manager's goal with wind over the water is to set the working day with confidence, knowing the marine window, the small-craft limit, and the load on moorings before conditions tighten. The aim is dive, departure, and mooring calls made early, not forced by a window that has already closed.

The twin surfaces the tightening early. It plays a wind speed and direction scenario forward over the resort and the water, reading the window, the gusts, and the mooring exposure against their limits. The value is the lead time to set departure windows and secure moorings while conditions still allow, keeping the operation ahead of the weather rather than chasing it.

## Water quality

How the water holds its clarity and condition under load, read by each persona that runs it.

### Environment Manager

The environment manager's goal with water quality is to keep the water the destination is built around in the condition guests came for, catching a developing problem while it can still be acted on rather than once it is established. The aim is to protect the asset before it visibly degrades.

The twin surfaces what the surface hides. It plays a wind, discharge, temperature, and flushing scenario forward across the lagoon, reading how dissolved oxygen, nutrients, turbidity, and temperature move together. The value is early sight of where the water is holding and where the balance is starting to tip, so the team can respond while a response still changes the outcome.

![Water quality, Marine Environment read.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-water-quality.jpg)

### Marine Operations Manager

The manager's goal with water quality is a clear call on whether the day's diving and swimming can go ahead, made before guests are in the water rather than after. The aim is to know a clarity drop is coming while the schedule can still adapt to it.

The twin gives that advance call. It plays a discharge and flushing scenario forward at the dive sites, reading clarity, salinity, temperature, and dissolved oxygen together. The value is surfacing a clarity drop ahead of time, so dive scheduling and site choice can be adjusted before the activity call is forced on the water.

![Water quality, Marine Operations read. Dive clarity 7.0m visibility below the activity threshold, DO low, activity limited.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-water-quality.jpg)

## Ocean current

How flow builds through the channels and the basin, read by each persona that runs it.

### Environment Manager

The manager's goal with ocean current is to understand how the basin keeps itself healthy, because residence time, mixing, and sediment transport are the quiet drivers behind most water-quality outcomes. The aim is to reason about the cause, not just manage the symptom.

The twin gives the underlying flow picture. It plays a tidal, wind, and seasonal scenario forward to read how the basin flushes and mixes and where sediment is carried. The value is explanatory: the team gains the why behind the water-quality readings, so interventions address the mechanism, like a slow-flushing basin holding onto what enters it, rather than chasing the effect.

![Ocean current, Marine Environment read.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-ocean-current.jpg)

## Wave

How offshore swell carries energy to the shore and the routes, read by each persona that runs it.

### Environment Manager

The manager's goal with wave energy is to stay ahead of shoreline change, knowing where the breaking energy concentrates and drives erosion before a season reshapes the coast. The target is protection planned around the stretches genuinely under pressure.

The twin shows where the energy lands. It plays an offshore swell scenario forward to the shoreline, reading where wave energy concentrates, where it breaks, and how it bears on the beach. The value is a clear view of the erosion driver ahead of the season, so the coast that actually needs watching is protected rather than guessed at.

## Coral reef

How heat stress builds on the reef against the bleaching scale, read by each persona that runs it.

### Environment Manager

The environment manager's goal with the reef is to protect both an asset and a responsibility, getting ahead of heat stress that builds invisibly long before bleaching is visible. The aim is to plan protection and rotation while the reef is still approaching the line, not once it has crossed it.

The twin makes the invisible legible and the long horizon manageable. It plays a heat-stress, water-quality, and disturbance scenario forward, reading bleaching risk on the established degree-heating-weeks scale and projecting accumulated stress over the chosen horizon. The value is separating a rising risk from an actual breach, so protection is a planned act rather than a reaction to damage already done.

![Coral reef, Marine Environment read.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-coral.jpg)

### Marine Operations Manager

For marine operations, the goal with the reef is dive-site management: getting ahead of the heat stress that pushes a site toward the point where it should be rested or closed. The aim is to protect both the reef and the operation that depends on it by acting before the trigger, not after.

The twin makes that timing possible. It plays a heat-stress scenario forward and reads bleaching risk at the dive sites against the closure trigger, with the anchor and boat pressure that compounds it. The value is the chance to plan rotation and protection while the risk is still building, so a site is rested deliberately rather than closed reactively.

![Coral reef, Marine Operations read. Bleaching risk 5.2 DHW approaching the closure trigger, reef health good, anchor risk low.](06-mo-lod3-simulation-coral.jpg)
