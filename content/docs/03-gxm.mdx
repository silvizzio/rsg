The Guest Experience Manager view exists to catch guest mobility problems before a guest ever feels one. In day-to-day operation a station can look perfectly healthy right up until the moment people are queuing and the complaints begin, and by then the problem is already a guest experience problem. This view is built to surface demand as it builds, so the manager can act while there is still time to keep anyone from waiting too long.

It does three things in particular. It shows demand rising before it turns into a queue. It gives a live picture of every guest on the move and the vehicle they are travelling in. And it ties a low satisfaction rating back to the specific trip that caused it, so a soft number finally has a cause attached. This is a read and oversight view: the manager watches, judges, and recommends here, while the actual dispatching is carried out in the Operations Manager view.

## What you see

At the wide view, the strip along the bottom is the guest mobility read for the whole destination. It is organised into the three phases of the guest journey, read left to right: what is building before a trip, what is moving during it, and what the trip left behind. Two station lists sit alongside, breaking the same picture down by where the demand is concentrating.

**Pre-trip** is the supply and demand picture before guests move.

| Field | Example | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Available vehicles | 6 of 12 | Vehicles free to assign now, and how many are Marina-assigned |
| Transfer variance | +18% | How far transfer demand is running above or below the planned 240 per hour |
| Booking requests | 412 | Mobility requests received in the last 60 minutes |

**Trip** is who is moving right now and in what.

| Field | Example | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle type mix | 22 buggy, 9 shuttle, 5 bus, 4 boat, 3 bike | The active fleet broken down by vehicle class |
| Guest per vehicle type | 22 buggy, 9 shuttle, 5 bus, 4 boat, 3 bike | How guests on the move split across those same classes |
| Guests travelling | 186 | Guests in transit right now |

**After-trip** is the outcome once guests have arrived.

| Field | Example | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Avg travel distance | 2.4 km | Typical distance covered per trip today |
| Avg travel time | 8.4 min | Typical door-to-door time today |
| Guest travel satisfaction | 92% satisfied | How guests are rating transfers |

Two station lists sit to the left of the strip. Request time by station ranks where requests are landing, here Marina Arrival, SLS Lobby, and EDITION Beach Club. Top stations ranks the busiest pickup points by request volume, here Marina, Turtle Bay Arrival, and Spine Corridor. Together they point to the part of the destination driving the destination-wide numbers.

## Reading demand across the destination

The read works at two levels. At the wide view the strip covers the whole destination across the three phases. Drop into a district, here the Marina, and the same read rescopes to that one area, which is usually where a destination-wide signal resolves into the place that is driving it.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Problem | A station can look healthy right up until guests are queuing, by which point it is already a guest experience problem. |
| Goal | Surface demand as it builds, across the destination and within a district, so it can be acted on before anyone waits too long. |
| Precondition | The guest experience feed is live: bookings, available vehicles, and trips in progress are reporting. |
| Steps | 1. Open the Guest Experience Manager from the persona bar.<br/>2. Read the destination mobility strip across the three phases: pre-trip, trip, and after-trip.<br/>3. Use the two station lists to see where demand is concentrating.<br/>4. Select a district to rescope the same read to one area. |
| Postcondition | The part of the destination driving the numbers is identified, early enough to act. |

## Following a guest trip

At the closest level the view follows a single guest trip through its three phases, and each phase surfaces its own recommended action. Each card carries the live feed for the flagged spot, opening it inside the card so the manager can confirm what is happening on the ground before acting, without leaving the card or hunting for the camera in the CCTV panel.

### Before the trip

Demand that used to be invisible until it became a queue is now legible while there is still room to add capacity.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Problem | An overwhelmed station only became visible once guests were already complaining. |
| Goal | See demand early enough to request extra capacity before the queue ever forms. |
| Precondition | The pre-trip read is live for the station: demand against capacity, free vehicles, and the oldest request age. |
| Steps | 1. The red card surfaces: demand 214 against a 150 capacity, 11 vehicles free, oldest request 8 minutes.<br/>2. Confirm the Marina feed inside the card.<br/>3. Request 6 more transfer boats from the Operations team, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | Extra capacity is requested before the queue forms; the dispatch itself is carried out in the Operations Manager view. |

![Close-up of the Marina, before the trip. The red card asks the Operations team for 6 more transfer boats, because demand has reached 214 against a capacity of 150, only 11 vehicles are free, and the oldest request has waited 8 minutes.](03-gxm-lod3-pre-trip.jpg)

### During the trip

With every guest in motion visible, a pinch point can be relieved while the trip is still under way rather than after it.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Problem | There was no clear picture of how many guests were actually moving at any moment. |
| Goal | See every guest in motion and the vehicle they are in, so a developing pinch point can be relieved mid-trip. |
| Precondition | The trip read is live: guests travelling and per-vehicle occupancy are reporting. |
| Steps | 1. The card surfaces: 318 guests on the move and the lead buggy already at 5 of 6 seats.<br/>2. Confirm the Spine Corridor feed inside the card.<br/>3. Send 2 buggies to the Spine Corridor, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | The pinch point is relieved while the trip is still under way. |

![Close-up of the Marina, during the trip. The card suggests sending 2 buggies to the Spine Corridor, because 318 guests are on the move and the lead buggy is already at 5 of 6 seats.](03-gxm-lod3-trip.jpg)

### After the trip

A low score that used to arrive with no explanation now points straight back to the leg that produced it.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Problem | Satisfaction scores arrived after the fact with no link to a cause. |
| Goal | Tie a low score straight back to the leg that produced it, so dissatisfaction can be traced rather than guessed at. |
| Precondition | The after-trip read is live: per-group satisfaction and travel time against target are reporting. |
| Steps | 1. The card flags a low-rated arrival: satisfaction averaging 4.1 of 5, one group at 3.6, travel time 8.4 minutes against a 6.3 target.<br/>2. Confirm the Marina Promenade feed, camera IB-03, inside the card.<br/>3. Open Route Review on the Marina to villas leg, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Postcondition | The dissatisfaction is traced to a specific leg and pattern, not a guess. |

## Simulations this view runs

The Guest Experience Manager runs the simulations that read the island through the guest: how they gather, how a space clears, how heat and exposure sit on the routes they walk, and how the fleet and network carry them. The simulation object is Humans, or Combined where a run also carries the vehicles guests share the site with. Each read below is summarised here and documented in full in the Simulation chapters.

| Category | Simulation | What it reads | What it drives | Read in full |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| People | Crowd | Density against the line where a space stops being comfortable | Meter flow or hold a release before a crush forms | [Crowd](/docs/08-simulation-people#crowd__guest-experience-manager) |
| People | Evacuation | How guest spaces clear and where people bunch | Design signage and capacity around the real bottleneck | [Evacuation](/docs/08-simulation-people#evacuation__guest-experience-manager) |
| People | Outdoor thermal comfort | Felt heat across the ground as shade shifts | Add shade or shift timing before an exposed stretch bites | [Outdoor thermal comfort](/docs/08-simulation-people#outdoor-thermal-comfort__guest-experience-manager) |
| People | Accessibility | Reach to amenities by walking effort | Design reach in rather than discover the gaps by chance | [Accessibility](/docs/08-simulation-people#accessibility__guest-experience-manager) |
| Traffic | Mobility | Transfer times and requests against the fleet | Adjust ahead of the surge, before anyone waits | [Mobility](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#mobility__guest-experience-manager) |
| Traffic | Shuttle network | Where reliance concentrates and where guests walk | Fix the leg that carries the network, not one route | [Shuttle network](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#shuttle-network__guest-experience-manager) |
| Traffic | Drop-off forecourt | Dwell, queue, and bay fill at the kerb | Set staging and kerb management ahead of the rush | [Drop-off forecourt](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#drop-off-forecourt__guest-experience-manager) |
| Transport | Wind | Where airflow funnels and accelerates between buildings | Soften an exposed corner through layout or routing | [Wind](/docs/10-simulation-transport#wind__guest-experience-manager) |
| Transport | Emissions and noise | How emission and noise carry across guest areas | Target routing and screening at the real sources | [Emissions and noise](/docs/10-simulation-transport#emissions-and-noise__guest-experience-manager) |
| Transport | Safety conflicts | Where guest and vehicle paths cross and clash | Put crossings and separation where the conflict is | [Safety conflicts](/docs/10-simulation-transport#safety-conflicts__guest-experience-manager) |

## Working in this view

1. Open the Guest Experience Manager from the persona bar.
2. Read the destination mobility strip at the wide view.
3. Select a district to inspect its stations and demand.
4. Open a station to follow its guest trip and read the recommended action.

<Callout type="note">
This is a read and oversight view. Dispatch and incident actions are carried out in the Operations Manager view.
</Callout>
