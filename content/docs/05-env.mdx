The Environment Manager view monitors air, wind, and water conditions across the destination. It combines live environmental readings with camera feeds from restricted areas to help you identify conditions that may affect guests.

Use the readings to assess changes in local conditions. The cameras also detect violations, such as swimming in a restricted area, and alert security when a violation is detected.

![The wide view of the destination, with environmental readings along the bottom: air quality, wind, and water.](05-env-lod1-1.jpg)

## Reading conditions across the destination

The destination overview shows conditions across the resort. Select a zone, such as Shura Marina, to see the same metrics for that area and locate the source of a wider change.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Why it matters | A change in air, wind, or water conditions may be missed until guests are affected. |
| What to check | Monitor local conditions so the team can issue advice or close an affected area. |
| Before you start | The environmental sensor feed is live: the air stations, the on-site wind station, and the water buoys are reporting. |
| What to do | 1. Sign in to the Environment Manager view using your assigned account.<br/>2. Read the destination strip: air quality and its pollutant breakdown, wind, and the water suite.<br/>3. Select a zone to see its local readings.<br/>4. Watch for any band moving toward its threshold. |
| Expected result | You have identified the zone where conditions are changing and can review a response. |

## Reading over time: sustainability and building systems

The panel shows both current environmental conditions and longer-term consumption trends. Use the analysis window at the top to switch the whole panel between NOW, MONTHLY, and YEARLY. This lets the sustainability team compare a building's use over time.

![The NOW panel charts current air quality, individual pollutants, wind, and water readings.](05-env-lod3-environmental-monitoring.jpg)

Scroll down to move from environmental conditions to electricity and water consumption, renewable share, and estimated CO2. Keeping these figures in the same panel lets you review a consumption trend alongside a current change in conditions.

![The lower NOW panel shows current electricity and water use, renewable share, and estimated CO2.](05-env-lod3-sustainability-bms-now.jpg)

![The MONTHLY panel shows the same metrics over the month.](05-env-lod3-sustainability-bms-monthly.jpg)

![The YEARLY panel lets the sustainability team track reductions over a full year.](05-env-lod3-sustainability-bms-yearly.jpg)

The lower panel groups the controls and readings as follows.

| Block | The question it answers | How it is organised | How to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analysis window | Current conditions or a longer-term trend? | One time-window control applies to all metrics | Compare current, monthly, and yearly figures for the selected site |
| Sustainability | Is this building using less than before? | Building-level readings show where consumption changes | Assess whether an efficiency initiative is reducing consumption |
| Building Management Systems | How much work are the building systems doing? | Live plant readings sit beside consumption data | Investigate consumption changes alongside cooling load and occupancy |

In MONTHLY and YEARLY, green indicates a favourable change and red an adverse change. The colour depends on what the change means: lower electricity use and a higher renewable share can both be favourable. NOW has no comparison tint because it has no previous window to compare with.

| Tint | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Green | Favourable change, such as lower electricity draw or higher renewable share |
| Red | Adverse movement against the target |



## Responding at a single site

At site level, recommended-action cards identify conditions that need attention. Each card describes the situation and includes a live camera feed for checking it. Select the primary action to respond or Remind me later to defer the card.

### Keeping monitoring live

An offline probe or degraded camera leaves a gap in monitoring, even if the other readings look normal. Check asset-health alerts to see where coverage needs restoring.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Why it matters | A failed sensor or degraded camera leaves a gap in monitoring. |
| What to check | Identify the affected assets and arrange to restore coverage. |
| Before you start | The environmental sensor feed is live and the asset health count is being tracked, here 43 monitoring assets. |
| What to do | 1. The Asset Offline card shows: water probe LG-04 offline for 6 minutes, reef camera RB-01 degraded, 41 of 43 assets reporting.<br/>2. Confirm the affected site, Lagoon East, on the live feed in the card.<br/>3. Dispatch a technician to restore coverage, or choose Remind me later to defer. |
| Expected result | A technician is dispatched to Lagoon East. Monitor the asset count as coverage is restored towards 43 of 43. |

![Close-up of Shura Marina. The red card sends a technician to Lagoon East because water probe LG-04 has been offline for 6 minutes and reef camera RB-01 is degraded, leaving 41 of 43 monitoring assets reporting.](05-env-lod3-monitoring-assets.jpg)

### Spotting a water anomaly

Water conditions can change before the difference is visible from shore. Compare a new reading with the baseline and supporting probe data before responding.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Why it matters | Water conditions can change before the difference is visible. |
| What to check | Compare the change with its baseline and probe readings, then send a team to investigate. |
| Before you start | The water buoys and probes are reporting, and a baseline colour index is established, here 94. |
| What to do | 1. The Water Anomaly card shows: colour index at 81 against a 94 baseline.<br/>2. Check the supporting readings: probe LG-04 confirms the reading, and air quality and wind are both nominal, so the signal is in the water rather than the air.<br/>3. Dispatch a marine team to Lagoon East, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Expected result | A marine team is sent to investigate Lagoon East, while the colour index remains available for tracking recovery. |

![Close-up of Shura Marina. The amber card sends a marine team to Lagoon East for a water anomaly, because the colour index has dropped to 81 from a 94 baseline, probe LG-04 confirms the reading, and air quality and wind are both nominal.](05-env-lod3-environmental-monitoring.jpg)

### Catching a violation

Restricted-area cameras help detect access that could put guests or the reef at risk. Check the live feed to confirm a detection and its location.

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Why it matters | Delayed detection of restricted-area access can expose guests and the reef to harm. |
| What to check | Confirm the camera detection and send security to the reported location. |
| Before you start | The violation-detection feed is running on the restricted-zone cameras. |
| What to do | 1. The Violation card reports: 2 swimmers in a restricted reef zone, alert 1 minute old, water quality reads 94.<br/>2. Confirm the detection on the live feed pinned inside the card.<br/>3. Dispatch security to the spot, SLS Red Sea, or defer with Remind me later. |
| Expected result | Security is dispatched to SLS Red Sea in response to the detection. |

![Close-up of Shura Marina. The red card calls security to SLS Red Sea for unauthorized swimmers, because 2 people are in a restricted reef zone, the alert is 1 minute old, and water quality reads 94.](05-env-lod3-safety-violation-detection.jpg)

## Simulations this view runs

Environment simulations examine heat exposure, renewable energy, emissions, and conditions in the water and on the reef. The table summarises the available simulations, with fuller explanations below and in the linked chapters.

| Category | Simulation | What it shows | Decisions it supports | Details |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| People | Outdoor thermal comfort | Heat exposure and the effect of passive cooling across the site | Target canopy and planting at areas with high heat exposure | [Outdoor thermal comfort](/docs/08-simulation-people#outdoor-thermal-comfort__environment-manager) |
| Traffic | Energy | Renewable share, emissions, curtailment, reserve | Assess demand against the renewable-energy target | [Energy](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#energy__environment-manager) |
| Transport | Emissions and noise | Where emissions and noise accumulate or dissipate | Prioritise the sources contributing most to exposure | [Emissions and noise](/docs/10-simulation-transport#emissions-and-noise__environment-manager) |
| Transport | Flood and storm surge | Where water reaches habitats and how sediment moves | Plan protection and recovery for affected areas | [Flood and storm surge](/docs/10-simulation-transport#flood-and-storm-surge__environment-manager) |
| Environment | Water quality | Changes in dissolved oxygen, nutrients, turbidity, and temperature | Investigate changes before water quality deteriorates | [Water quality](/docs/11-simulation-environment#water-quality__environment-manager) |
| Environment | Ocean current | How the basin flushes, mixes, and carries sediment | Address the mechanism behind water-quality outcomes | [Ocean current](/docs/11-simulation-environment#ocean-current__environment-manager) |
| Environment | Wave | Where breaking energy concentrates and drives erosion | Identify coastal areas needing protection | [Wave](/docs/11-simulation-environment#wave__environment-manager) |
| Environment | Coral reef | Bleaching risk over the forecast period in degree heating weeks | Plan protection before the bleaching-risk threshold is reached | [Coral reef](/docs/11-simulation-environment#coral-reef__environment-manager) |

### People

**Outdoor thermal comfort.** The Environment Manager uses this simulation to assess heat exposure across the site. It shows how shading, surface choices, and passive cooling affect outdoor comfort as conditions change. Review where heat exposure concentrates, how much ground remains shaded, and where passive cooling is insufficient. These results help target canopy, planting, and surface changes at the areas that need them. [Read in full](/docs/08-simulation-people#outdoor-thermal-comfort__environment-manager)

![Outdoor thermal comfort, Environment view. Site shade availability against the heat-stress footprint, exposure rising as midday peaks.](05-env-lod3-simulation-outdoor-thermal-comfort.jpg)

### Traffic

**Energy.** The Environment Manager uses the energy simulation to assess progress towards the renewable-energy target under the selected demand and supply conditions. The view also helps identify when renewable generation would go unused while reserves decline. Review renewable share, emissions, curtailment, and reserve together. They show the environmental consequences of the scenario and help the team assess whether dispatch and reserve choices support the target. [Read in full](/docs/09-simulation-traffic#energy__environment-manager)

![Energy, Environment view.](05-env-lod3-simulation-energy.jpg)

### Transport

**Emissions and noise.** The Environment Manager uses this simulation to assess emissions and noise across the whole site. It shows where exposure accumulates and where it dissipates under the selected conditions. Review the affected areas alongside their sources. This helps direct mitigation towards the sources contributing most to the problem and towards areas intended to remain clean and quiet. [Read in full](/docs/10-simulation-transport#emissions-and-noise__environment-manager)

![Emissions and noise, Environment view.](05-env-lod3-simulation-transport-emission-and-noise.jpg)

**Flood and storm surge.** The Environment Manager uses this simulation to assess habitats in the path of a flood or storm surge. It considers both inundation and the movement of sediment through sensitive areas. The forecast shows where water reaches, how levels change, and which ecosystems are exposed. Use it to plan protection and post-event recovery around the areas likely to be most affected. [Read in full](/docs/10-simulation-transport#flood-and-storm-surge__environment-manager)

![Flood and storm surge, Environment view.](05-env-lod3-simulation-transport-flood-and-storm-surge.jpg)

### Environment

**Water quality.** Use the water-quality simulation to examine how lagoon conditions could change under a selected wind, discharge, temperature, and flushing scenario. The forecast shows dissolved oxygen, nutrients, turbidity, and temperature together. Review where those readings remain stable and where they begin to move out of balance, so the team can investigate a developing problem before visible deterioration. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#water-quality__environment-manager)

![Water quality, Environment view.](05-env-lod3-simulation-environment-water-quality.jpg)

**Ocean current.** Use the current simulation to understand how water moves through the basin and how that movement affects water quality. The relevant factors include residence time, mixing, and sediment transport. The forecast models tide, wind, and seasonal conditions to show flushing, mixing, and sediment movement. These results help explain water-quality readings, such as a slow-flushing area retaining material that enters it, and support responses aimed at the cause. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#ocean-current__environment-manager)

![Ocean current, Environment view.](05-env-lod3-simulation-environment-ocean-current.jpg)

**Wave.** The Environment Manager uses the wave simulation to assess where wave energy could contribute to shoreline erosion. The forecast follows offshore swell towards the shore and shows where energy concentrates and waves break. Use it to identify stretches of coast that need monitoring or protection before the season's conditions develop. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#wave__environment-manager)

![Wave, Environment view.](05-env-lod3-simulation-environment-wave.jpg)

**Coral reef.** Use the coral-reef simulation to assess how heat stress, water quality, and disturbance could affect the reef over the selected forecast period. The forecast reports bleaching risk on the degree-heating-weeks scale and shows how stress accumulates. Compare rising risk with the relevant threshold when planning protection or site rotation, before a breach occurs. [Read in full](/docs/11-simulation-environment#coral-reef__environment-manager)

![Coral reef, Environment view.](05-env-lod3-simulation-environment-coral.jpg)

## Working in this view

1. Sign in to the Environment Manager view using your assigned account.
2. Read the destination air, wind, and water strip at the wide view.
3. Select a zone to inspect its environmental readings.
4. Open the site associated with a card, check its live feed, and choose whether to respond or defer.
